# Customer Churn Prediction

Predicting which customers are likely to stop using a service, using the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset. Built as an end-to-end classification project: EDA → preprocessing → model training → evaluation.

## Overview

- **Dataset:** [IBM Telco Customer Churn](https://github.com/IBM/telco-customer-churn-on-icp4d) — 7,043 customers, 21 features (demographics, account info, service usage)
- **Task:** Binary classification — will a customer churn (`Yes`/`No`)?
- **Models:** Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost
- **Best result:** Random Forest, **ROC-AUC 0.843**, Recall 0.781

## Key Findings

- Overall churn rate is **26.5%**
- **Contract type is the single strongest churn driver** — month-to-month customers churn at **42.7%** vs. just **2.8%** for two-year contracts
- **Tenure matters a lot** — churned customers average 18 months on the service vs. 37.6 months for retained customers, meaning risk is concentrated in the first year
- **Fiber optic internet customers churn more** (41.9%) than DSL customers (19.0%), despite paying more on average — suggesting price sensitivity or service quality issues in that segment

## Results

| Model | Accuracy | Recall | Precision | F1 | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Random Forest** | 0.759 | 0.781 | 0.531 | 0.632 | **0.843** |
| Logistic Regression | 0.739 | **0.783** | 0.505 | 0.614 | 0.842 |
| XGBoost | 0.758 | 0.778 | 0.530 | 0.631 | 0.842 |

All three models perform similarly (~0.84 AUC), indicating the churn signal is well captured by the engineered features rather than being especially model-sensitive. Recall was prioritized (via `class_weight="balanced"` / `scale_pos_weight`) since missing an at-risk customer is usually costlier to a business than a wasted retention offer.

## Methodology

1. **Data cleaning:** `TotalCharges` had 11 blank values, all belonging to brand-new customers (`tenure = 0`); coerced to numeric and filled with 0.
2. **EDA:** churn broken down by contract type, tenure, internet service, and monthly charges to identify the strongest risk factors.
3. **Preprocessing:** `ColumnTransformer` combining `StandardScaler` (numeric features) and `OneHotEncoder` (categorical features), wrapped in a single `sklearn.Pipeline` per model to prevent data leakage between train/test.
4. **Train/test split:** 80/20, stratified on the target to preserve the 26.5% churn rate in both sets.
5. **Models:** Logistic Regression, Random Forest (300 trees), XGBoost (300 trees) — all class-weighted to handle the moderate imbalance.
6. **Evaluation:** Accuracy, Recall, Precision, F1, and ROC-AUC, plus confusion matrices and ROC curves for visual comparison.

## Business Recommendations

1. **Push month-to-month customers onto annual contracts** via discounts or incentives — this is the highest-leverage retention lever available.
2. **Focus retention efforts on the first 12 months** of the customer lifecycle, where churn risk is highest.
3. **Investigate the fiber optic segment** — higher churn despite higher spend points to a service quality or competitive pricing issue worth a deeper look.
